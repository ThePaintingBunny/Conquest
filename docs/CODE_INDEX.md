### ai/bot

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `ai/bot/activation.js` | **Built-in opponent: one activation.** Lists every sensible line for the activating regiment (Charge, March then Charge, March twice, Volley, Aim, Reform, Clash with or without Inspire, Combat Reform, Withdraw, hold), scores where each line leaves it, and returns the first step of the best one. After each step the activation is planned again from the new situation, so a failed Charge or a lucky Volley is taken into account. |
| `ai/bot/army.js` | **Built-in opponent: its army.** Builds a legal army for the built-in opponent with the army builder's own rules: characters with full warbands (a Mainstay regiment the character can join first, then Mainstay and Restricted choices), checked with the builder's warband and army checks, as close to the points limit as it gets. Several random tries; the best one wins: full on points, and built to be on the table early (few Heavy regiments, which only arrive from round 3), with enough regiments and regiments a sensible size (spare points buy extra stands). |
| `ai/bot/character.js` | **Built-in opponent: characters and spells.** Picks a character's Draw Events and its one action: a spell (scored from the spell's hits, healing or effect and the chance to cast it), an ability, a Duel with good odds, or nothing. |
| `ai/bot/damage.js` | **Built-in opponent: expected damage.** How many wounds one attack is expected to do, worked out from the game's own attack groups, hit numbers, Defense and Morale (no dice are rolled). The attacker is placed in contact on the chosen facing in a copy of the table, so every rule that looks at the arc, support or Inspiration still applies. |
| `ai/bot/movement.js` | **Built-in opponent: movement.** Predicts where a March ends exactly the way the March executor will carry it out (wheel to face the point, then straight on, stopping 1″ short of enemies and at anything in the way), and lists the places worth marching to: zones, enemies, the flanks and rear of enemies, and away from the worst threat. |
| `ai/bot/plan.js` | **Built-in opponent: the army's plan for the round.** Before its regiments act, the built-in opponent decides what each one is for this round:   - an objective zone to take or hold;   - an enemy regiment to fight;   - a flanking move: a fast regiment goes around an enemy regiment to charge its flank or rear;   - a guard: standing beside a friend that is fighting or about to, on the side an enemy could charge its flank from;   - a lane: holding open ground with no objective at the left, centre or right of the table, so the line is wide     and the enemy has to spread out too. All regiments are planned together (the assignment method), so they spread over the table instead of all heading for the nearest prize, and a zone or target only gets as many regiments as it needs. Regiments still in reserve count too, so the ones on the table leave them a job. |
| `ai/bot/stack.js` | **Built-in opponent: the Command Stack.** Orders the cards by what acting early is worth, in points, not by how threatened a regiment is:   - saved: how much of the risk it faces it can remove by acting before the enemy (moving out of reach,     turning to face, striking first in a fight). A cheap regiment that cannot escape saves nothing, so it goes     late and is left as the sacrifice.   - chance: an attack open to it now that the enemy may take away by acting first.   - react: going late lets a quiet regiment answer what the enemy did. Each regiment gets a value for every slot (how many enemy activations come before it), and the order with the best total is found exactly (the assignment method). A character goes just before its own regiment, so the regiment acts straight after as its Entourage. |
| `ai/bot/threats.js` | **Built-in opponent: threats.** Who can reach whom. For any regiment in any pose: which enemies could Charge or Volley it in their next activation, with what chance, into which facing, and what that would cost in points. The same measure, turned around, says how much a pose threatens the enemy. |
| `ai/bot/values.js` | **Built-in opponent: values.** Everything the built-in opponent weighs is measured in army points: a regiment's worth, a wound, a zone, a Victory Point. The weights live in BOT so they can be tuned in one place. |
